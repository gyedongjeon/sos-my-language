## Request

Bring `sos-my-english` and `sos-my-german` together in one app, `sos-my-language`, as the first
branch of the repository: start from a copy of `sos-my-english`, ask on the first start which
language to practise (English or German), and build it so that more languages can be added
later. Said during the work: the two apps have almost no users yet, so they are merged now,
before migrating their databases becomes a burden; a learner may study several languages; the
history and the corrections should show the language being practised only, like the videos; tests of
upgrades from old databases are not wanted in a new app; and the menus and descriptions should
follow the learner's own language too, before the branch is merged.

## Changes

- **Copied from `sos-my-english` v0.8.1**, then renamed: `SOS My Language`, `sos-my-language`,
  the `SOS_MY_LANGUAGE_` environment variables, the app id, the update repository, and the
  release asset names. The version starts again at `0.1.0` in all three packages. The worklogs
  and the screenshots of `sos-my-english` were left out; screenshots of this app can follow.
- **A language is described in one place.** `backend/app/languages/` has a package for each
  language (`english/`, `german/`) that defines a `Language` (`base.py`): its code and names, its
  Whisper model, subtitle codes, abbreviations, how short forms and number words are heard, its
  voices, the opening line of each built-in topic, its 100 sentence patterns, and the examples
  its prompts use. The rest of the backend asks `current_language()` and names no language. What
  was German-only in `sos-my-german` (the "du"/"Sie" rule, "ß" as "ss", "geht's", German
  abbreviations and patterns, the Piper voices) moved there as it was.
- **Choosing the language.** `GET /api/languages` lists the languages and which one is chosen;
  `PUT /api/language` chooses one. None is chosen on the first start: the frontend shows only a
  page that asks, and nothing is downloaded before (`POST /api/models/download` starts nothing
  until then). Afterwards the language can be changed with a selector next to the app's name and
  under Settings.
- **One database, with the language on every row that is learned.** Asked during the work and
  decided for one `app.db` over a database file for each language. The tables `topics`,
  `conversations`, `videos`, `pattern_progress`, `own_patterns`, `pattern_mistakes`,
  `shadowing_mistakes`, and `shadowing_attempts` have a `target` column with the language's code,
  and titles, video ids, and patterns are unique within a language. The built-in topics are seeded
  for every language, with that language's opening lines. The schema is written out as it is, in
  one script: with no data of the earlier apps to carry over (also decided during the work), the
  column-by-column upgrades of `sos-my-english` and their tests were removed.
- **What is shown.** Topics, videos (with their search), patterns (with progress and own
  patterns), the history, and the corrections are those of the language being practised. History
  and Corrections first listed every language, with the language on each entry and a switch to
  it on opening; after trying the app this was asked to be like the videos, one language at a
  time. A conversation opened by its link still goes over to its language.
- **Speech.** Whisper is told the language and loads the language's model (`small.en` for English,
  `small` otherwise); an English-only model named in `WHISPER_MODEL` is used for English alone.
  Text-to-speech has both programs: Kokoro for English and Piper (`piper-tts`, added as a
  dependency) for German. The voice is chosen for each language; the voice list says each voice's
  size and whether it is downloaded, as `sos-my-german` did, and the download's size follows the
  chosen voice.
- **Download progress of a second language.** The speech recognition's progress counted every
  file in `whisper/`, so with the model of one language there, the next showed "803 / 486 MB".
  Found while trying the app; it now counts the files of the model in use, wherever Hugging Face
  keeps them (a model's own folder or the one shared by all), and the files still on their way.
- **Prompts** name the language being practised in place of "English", and all of them now carry
  the rule `sos-my-german` added after live runs: texts for the learner are asked for in the
  learner's own language "whatever language the conversation was in", with only quoted words in
  the language practised. The review's phrase field is `expression` (was `english` and `german`).
- **Subtitles.** Stage directions such as "(Laughter)" and "(Gelächter)" are recognized in any
  alphabet (a word or two in brackets with only its first letter a capital), and the search of
  saved sentences ignores the case of every letter, as in `sos-my-german`.
- **Frontend.** The pages say the language's name where they said "English"; the fallback
  browser voice uses the language's locale; Settings has "Language to practise", the voice sample
  of the language, and the download note of a voice.
- **The pages in the learner's language.** The feedback language is now "Your language": the
  pages are shown in it as well as corrections explained in it. `frontend/src/i18n/` has every
  text of the pages by a key (`en.ts`, about 230 of them) and the same keys in Korean (`ko.ts`);
  pages and stores call `t('key')`, with names filled in and "one | many" forms. The built-in
  topics (titles and situations), categories, pattern notes, and voice descriptions, which the
  backend keeps in English (the situation is what the LLM is told), are shown through
  `tc(text)` from `content.ko.json`. A small helper was written instead of adding a library:
  the keys are checked by TypeScript, and there is nothing a library would add here. English
  and Korean only, decided during the work: translations into the other seventeen feedback
  languages could not be checked by anyone, and each can be added later as two files; until then
  those languages get the English pages. The first start loads the learner's language (from the
  browser) before asking which language to practise, so the question is asked in it. Not
  translated: the backend's error messages and the desktop app's dialogs.
- **Kept from `sos-my-english` only**, since it was ahead of `sos-my-german`: asking again for
  missing translations, and the rest of v0.7 to v0.8.1.
- **Docs**: `README.md` and `README.ko.md` describe the app, the languages, and how to add one;
  `AGENTS.md` names the project and the rule that code outside `languages/` names no language.
- **Not done**: pages in languages other than English and Korean; the app icon is still the one of `sos-my-english`; importing the data of the two
  earlier apps (decided against); a third language.

## Verification

- `cd backend && uv run pytest`: 278 passed, 16 live tests deselected. `tests/test_page_texts.py`
  checks that every built-in text has its entry in each `content.<language>.json` of the frontend. `tests/test_languages.py`
  is new (35 tests): what every listed language must have, the first start, choosing, that each
  language has its own topics, videos, patterns, progress, voice, history, and corrections, the prompts of each language, German words as heard, and both voice programs.
- `cd frontend && npm test`: 140 passed (the new `src/i18n/index.test.ts` among them: every key is
  there in Korean with the same names to fill in); `npm run build` (type check and build) succeeded.
- `cd desktop && npm test`: 48 passed; `npx tsc --noEmit` succeeded.
- The server was started on a new data folder and looked at in a headless Chrome: the first start
  shows only the language question; with German chosen, the home page, Settings (German voices,
  "114 MB to download"), and the history; opening the English conversation by its link while German was chosen went over to
  English. (This was before the history was limited to one language; that change was checked by
  the tests only.)
- With a browser set to Korean on a new data folder: the language question, the home page (topics
  and categories), the patterns of German, and Settings were all in Korean. A scan of the
  templates found no text or label left as fixed English besides the app's name.
- With the real models in that folder (and `WHISPER_MODEL=tiny`, to keep the download small): the
  Kokoro voice rendered an English line and Whisper wrote it back; after going over to German the
  Piper voice was downloaded (113,900,076 bytes), rendered "Guten Morgen! Herzlich willkommen. Was
  darf es für Sie sein?", and Whisper wrote back "Guten Morgen. Herzlich Willkommen. Was darf es
  für Sie sein?". Both voices worked in the same server process.
- `uv run --env-file .env pytest -m live` (Gemini): 16 passed. Four of them are new and practise
  German with Korean as the learner's language: the partner answers in German and explains in
  Korean, the review is in Korean with German expressions, pattern sentences have German answers,
  and the first line of an own topic is German.
- `cd desktop && npm run dist` built `SOS My Language-0.1.0-arm64.dmg` (128 MB); it was not opened.
- Not verified: clicking through the pages by hand (the picker's buttons, the selector, and
  practising a correction of another language were covered by store tests and by the API, not by
  clicks); the desktop app and a packaged build; Windows; CI.
